## 1. Two different coverage concepts

**Match-statistics coverage** is the share of matches whose own serve/return statistics are present and valid. **Prediction coverage** is the share of feature rows (pre-match snapshots) for which useful *prior* statistics exist for the players. A match can have perfect statistics and still be predicted from an empty history (e.g. a debutant), and vice versa.

## 7. Selection bias from uneven coverage

- **Era:** no tour-level statistics exist before 1991. Every player starts the validation period with an empty statistical history, so early-1990s features are mostly prior-driven, and careers that began before 1991 are only partly observed.
- **Event type:** Davis Cup matches are far less often covered than tour events (see by-level table). A player's history therefore over-represents tour-level matches, and players who mainly play Davis Cup or lower tiers (not in the default pool) have thinner and less representative histories.
- **Survivorship:** players accumulate statistics by staying on tour; strong players reach later rounds and generate more points. Sample size is correlated with ability, so `has_stats_history` and point counts carry information beyond the rates themselves, and missingness is not at random.
- **Retirements and flags:** matches with a retirement, a walkover or flagged statistics never contribute. Retirements are not random (injury, fatigue), so their exclusion slightly favours healthy-match data.
- **Population prior:** the prior is the rate of the observed population, which over-represents tour regulars.
